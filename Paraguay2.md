Paraguay Banking Crisis: Collapse and Intervention of Ueno Bank
Overview
Paraguay’s Central Bank (Banco Central del Paraguay - BCP) placed Ueno Bank, the country's largest digital lender, into regulatory resolution following an acute capital and solvency deficit. The sudden freeze has cut off approximately 3 million customers from digital banking services and triggered severe political fallout, including an impeachment motion against President Santiago Peña.
What We Know
 * Regulatory Intervention and Shutdown:
   * The BCP board voted unanimously to place Ueno Bank into resolution after its solvency ratio fell below 50% of the statutory minimum.
   * All services, mobile applications, cards, and electronic transfers have been suspended while appointed administrators step in.
 * Scale of Depositor and Financial Impact:
   * Roughly 3 million customers are currently unable to access their balances.
   * Public funds are heavily exposed, including approximately $380 million in state social security and health fund (IPS) deposits, as well as capital from state utility ANDE and binational entities (e.g., Itaipú).
   * About 32,000 retirees receiving monthly pensions through Ueno Bank must be reassigned to other banking institutions.
 * Deposit Guarantee Scheme:
   * Regulators estimate that approximately 97% of individual retail depositors fall within the legal payout cap protected by Paraguay's statutory deposit guarantee fund (Fondo de Garantía de Depósitos).
 * Executive Arrests and Underlying Fraud Allegations:
   * Prosecutors issued arrest warrants for 8 bank executives (with at least 6 detained) on allegations of fraud, breach of trust, and improper conduct during a solvency crisis.
   * Prior regulatory reviews flagged illicit insider/related-party lending, with funds allegedly funneled into businesses tied to the controlling shareholder group (Grupo Vázquez).
 * Political Repercussions:
   * President Santiago Peña previously held shares in the business ecosystem tied to Ueno Bank.
   * BCP President Carlos Carvallo, who led Ueno Bank before his appointment to the monetary authority, submitted his resignation at President Peña's request.
   * Opposition lawmakers filed a formal impeachment request (juicio político) against President Peña over alleged conflict of interest and failure of supervisory oversight.
What Remains Uncertain / Unknown
 * Recovery of Public and Uninsured Funds: Whether institutional entities (such as IPS) and depositors holding balances above the statutory deposit guarantee threshold will recover their money during asset liquidation or portfolio exclusion.
 * Timeline for Depositor Liquidity: The concrete schedule and payout logistics for when insured retail customers will physically receive their funds or migrate to other lenders.
 * Degree of Sector Contagion: Whether anxiety outside branches will trigger secondary deposit runs or systemic liquidity pressure on other domestic financial institutions, despite BCP stating the case is isolated.
 * Legislative Outcome: Whether the impeachment push against President Peña will gather enough votes, as the ruling Colorado Party holds a working majority across both houses of Congress.

# Sectorial Cyber Resilience & Credit Intelligence Report
## Slide Objectives & Content Validation Framework

### Overall Report Objective

The report aims to equip Senior Credit Officers with sector-specific intelligence to understand cyber and operational resilience exposures, interpret weaknesses identified through client questionnaires, and assess their potential implications for business continuity and financial resilience.

The report supports informed, evidence-based discussions with corporate borrowers. It is not intended to constitute a formal cybersecurity audit, compliance assessment or independent verification of borrower controls.

---

## Slide 01 — Sector Intelligence Cover

**Objective:**

Establish the sector, geographical scope, reporting period and intelligence classification of the report.

**Validation criteria:**

The slide should clearly identify the sector being analysed, the geographical coverage, reporting date, intelligence cutoff and document classification.

## Slide 02 — Executive Sector Risk Snapshot

**Objective:**

Provide an immediate view of the sector's inherent exposure across Fraud, Cyber/ICT, Operational Resilience/TPRM and Governance/Operating Model, highlighting principal risks and credit implications.

**Validation criteria:**

The slide should communicate the four sector exposure ratings, their principal justifications, the most significant risks and their potential implications for corporate credit risk.

The reader should understand the overall sector risk profile without having to consult subsequent slides.

## Slide 03 — Sector Anatomy: Critical Dependencies & Credit Exposure

**Objective:**

Identify the critical business operations, technologies, infrastructure and third-party dependencies whose disruption could materially affect the sector's operations and financial resilience.

**Validation criteria:**

The slide should identify the sector's essential business activities, critical technology dependencies, important third parties and potential single points of failure.

It should explain how disruptions to these dependencies could affect business continuity and financial performance.

## Slide 04 — Priority Cyber Threats & Credit Exposure

**Objective:**

Identify and prioritise the most significant cyber threats affecting the sector, explaining attack methods, exposed activities, emerging trends and potential business consequences.

**Validation criteria:**

The slide should identify the principal cyber threat scenarios, relevant attack methods, affected business activities and observed threat developments.

It should demonstrate why these threats are particularly relevant to the sector and explain their potential operational and credit implications.

## Slide 05 — Geopolitical Cyber Exposure

**Objective:**

Assess how geopolitical tensions, state-linked cyber activity and strategic dependencies influence the sector's threat landscape and potential for widespread disruption.

**Validation criteria:**

The slide should identify geopolitical developments relevant to the sector, associated cyber threat actors or campaigns, exposed geographies and potential disruption pathways.

It should clearly demonstrate the connection between geopolitical factors, sector-specific cyber exposure and potential business or financial consequences.

## Slide 06 — Major Cyber Incidents & Lessons Learned

**Objective:**

Examine documented cyberattacks to demonstrate how threats have materialised, which weaknesses were exploited and what operational, financial and regulatory consequences followed.

**Validation criteria:**

The slide should present significant and relevant historical incidents, explaining the attack mechanisms, confirmed security weaknesses, resulting business disruption and documented consequences.

The analysis should extract practical lessons applicable to the sector rather than merely describe incidents.

## Slide 07 — Critical Cyber Resilience Capabilities & Control Weaknesses

**Objective:**

Identify the security and resilience capabilities most important to the sector, using incident evidence to explain why specific control weaknesses could increase operational exposure.

**Validation criteria:**

The slide should identify critical cybersecurity and resilience capabilities, the threats they mitigate and the potential consequences of their absence or failure.

Where possible, findings should be supported by documented incidents or credible sector intelligence.

The slide should distinguish between inherent sector exposure and weaknesses in individual organisations.

## Slide 08 — Regulatory & Supervisory Resilience Expectations

**Objective:**

Explain the principal cyber and operational resilience expectations imposed by relevant authorities, including their implications for business continuity, regulatory exposure and financial resilience.

**Validation criteria:**

The slide should identify relevant regulatory authorities, applicable frameworks and key resilience expectations across the jurisdictions covered.

It should explain how material shortcomings could result in operational, regulatory or financial consequences.

Regulatory applicability must be clearly distinguished from general industry good practices.

## Slide 09 — Cyber Incidents & Financial Impact

**Objective:**

Quantify the observed financial consequences of cyber incidents using credible sector statistics, historical losses, disruption durations, recovery costs and financial trends.

**Validation criteria:**

The slide should present meaningful financial indicators supported by credible evidence, including documented costs, operational disruption, recovery expenditure and relevant historical trends.

It should distinguish actual reported losses from estimates, assumptions and industry averages.

The reader should understand the potential financial scale of cyber incidents affecting the sector.

## Slide 10 — Cyber-to-Credit Risk Transmission & Stress Testing

**Objective:**

Demonstrate how plausible cyber disruptions could affect revenue, profitability, liquidity, working capital and debt-servicing capacity, using illustrative financial stress scenarios.

**Validation criteria:**

The slide should clearly explain the mechanisms through which cyber incidents can translate into financial deterioration and potential credit exposure.

Stress scenarios should demonstrate the consequences of different disruption durations or severities, with clearly stated assumptions.

The slide should distinguish hypothetical sector scenarios from conclusions about the creditworthiness of individual borrowers.

## Slide 11 — Questionnaire-to-Sector Intelligence Mapping

**Objective:**

Connect potential weaknesses identified through the client questionnaire to sector-specific threats, documented incidents, resilience expectations and potential financial consequences, enabling Credit Officers to understand their significance.

**Validation criteria:**

The slide should demonstrate a clear relationship between questionnaire findings, relevant sector intelligence, documented attack evidence and potential operational or financial consequences.

It should help Senior Credit Officers understand why a particular weakness matters in the sector and which borrower-specific factors could influence its significance.

The analysis should support informed discussion without treating questionnaire responses as independently verified findings.

## Slide 12 — Borrower Engagement & Credit Escalation Guide

**Objective:**

Translate the intelligence into practical client discussion questions, resilience improvement opportunities, early warning indicators and proportionate follow-up or escalation actions.

**Validation criteria:**

The slide should provide clear, non-technical questions that Senior Credit Officers can use during client discussions.

It should identify relevant warning indicators, opportunities for resilience improvement and circumstances warranting additional information, monitoring or specialist escalation.

The recommendations should be proportionate and avoid turning the credit discussion into a formal cybersecurity audit.

# Overall Presentation Validation

The complete presentation should achieve four objectives.

### 1. Establish Sector Exposure — Slides 01–03

Explain the sector's inherent risk profile, operating model and critical business dependencies.

### 2. Establish Threat Evidence — Slides 04–07

Demonstrate the principal cyber threats, geopolitical drivers, historical attack evidence and relevant security or resilience weaknesses.

### 3. Establish Resilience Expectations and Financial Consequences — Slides 08–10

Explain applicable resilience expectations, documented financial impacts and plausible cyber-to-credit risk transmission mechanisms.

### 4. Enable Informed Borrower Engagement — Slides 11–12

Connect intelligence to the client questionnaire and provide practical guidance for proportionate, evidence-based credit discussions.

# Instructions for LLM-Based Slide Review

For each slide, evaluate whether its content achieves the stated objective and satisfies the associated validation criteria.

Assess the slide against the following dimensions:

1. **Objective alignment:** Does the content directly address the intended objective?
2. **Completeness:** Are the essential elements sufficiently covered?
3. **Evidence quality:** Are material claims supported by relevant, credible and traceable evidence?
4. **Analytical relevance:** Does the content provide sector-specific intelligence rather than generic cybersecurity information?
5. **Credit relevance:** Are operational or financial implications clearly explained where appropriate?
6. **Clarity:** Is the message understandable to a Senior Credit Officer without specialist cybersecurity knowledge?
7. **Consistency:** Does the slide complement the overall narrative without unnecessary duplication or contradictions?

## Expected Evaluation Output

For each slide, provide:

- **Slide number and title**
- **Objective achieved:** Yes / Partially / No
- **Score:** 1–5
- **What works:** Key strengths of the existing content
- **What is missing:** Important gaps against the objective
- **Recommended improvements:** Specific, actionable changes
- **Evidence requiring validation:** Unsupported or uncertain claims, figures or conclusions

Use the following scoring scale:

- **5 — Fully meets objective:** Clear, complete, relevant and adequately evidenced.
- **4 — Largely meets objective:** Strong content with minor improvements required.
- **3 — Partially meets objective:** Relevant but missing important information or analytical depth.
- **2 — Weak alignment:** Significant gaps or insufficient evidence.
- **1 — Does not meet objective:** The slide fails to communicate its intended message.

Do not award a high score simply because a slide is visually appealing or contains substantial information.

Evaluate the substance of the intelligence, its relevance to the sector and its usefulness to Senior Credit Officers.

**Final instruction:** Identify the three most important improvements required across the entire presentation, prioritising those that would most strengthen its value in the corporate credit process.


What happened ?
 * Supply Chain Attack: The authorized reseller CryptoBilis underwent an ownership change around March 2026, which was not communicated to Ledger.
 * Hardware Tampering: The attackers physically compromised Ledger hardware wallets.
 * Technical Methodology: Implants with cellular capabilities (LTE/eSIM) were hidden behind the device screens. These modules captured the 24-word recovery phrase directly from the screen during setup and transmitted it over cellular networks, bypassing the user's internet connection.
 * Security Bypass: The implants were designed to monitor and extract data quietly without triggering Ledger's security protocols, including the "Genuine Check".
What is the Impact ?
 * Financial Losses: Estimated stolen assets range from $72 million to approximately $92.9 million USD.
 * Scope: The attack affected roughly 311 to 315 wallets across multiple blockchains (Tron, Bitcoin, Ethereum, BNB Chain, and Polygon). In one instance, 111 Bitcoin wallets were emptied within a single block, and 25 Tron wallets approved a spender within three seconds. One user lost 80 BTC.
 * Asset Laundering: Stolen funds were moved into anonymizing services like Tornado Cash.
What is being done & next step
 * Immediate Actions: Ledger is actively investigating and requested CryptoBilis to suspend all sales and shipments of Ledger devices on October 9, 2026. Tether froze approximately $10 million in stolen USDT.
 * Security Recommendations: Customers who purchased from CryptoBilis in the past 90 days are advised not to initialize new devices. Those already using affected devices should migrate their funds to a new wallet with a newly generated recovery phrase.
 * Ongoing Investigation: The investigation remains focused on devices distributed by this specific Southeast Asian reseller channel. There is currently no evidence that Ledger's own core systems or direct sales were compromised.

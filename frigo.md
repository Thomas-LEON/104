# Incident Report — Suspected Cyberattacks Against Refrigeration Systems at European Supermarkets


**Status as of 2 November 2026.** Refrigerators and freezers at several European retail chains experienced disruptions confirmed or suspected to have been caused by cyberattacks. The BBC headline refers to **seven countries**, while other publications report a narrower geographical scope. It has not been established that all incidents belong to the same campaign. 
## 1. What happened?


A new wave of incidents was reported on **2 November 2026**, including in Belgium, Luxembourg, Sweden, Ireland and Estonia. Refrigeration control systems experienced temperature changes that compromised food storage. This wave follows similar incidents affecting hundreds of French supermarkets in October; similarities between the incidents do not establish a common origin. 
In Estonia, attackers accessed the control computer of an older Danfoss refrigeration system and set a freezer’s temperature to **+50°C**. The affected supermarket chain has not been publicly named. 


## 2. What was the business impact?


- **Stock losses:** Fresh and frozen products had to be withdrawn and destroyed. Reported food losses amount to several million euros, although no consolidated financial assessment is available in the reviewed sources. Approximately **40 Delhaize stores** in Belgium and Luxembourg were reportedly affected. 
- **Business disruption:** Some stores temporarily closed. In Ireland, SuperValu withdrew products from **13 stores**.
- **Impact varied by location:** In the Estonian case described by Kitman Coldtech, intervention within ten minutes of the incident being reported prevented damage. 


## 3. What is being done, and what is the next step?

### Status as of 2 November 2026


**Containment and investigations are ongoing.** Sabcobel, the refrigeration systems provider for Delhaize, confirmed a cyberattack and mobilised teams to disconnect affected units. SuperValu is continuing its investigation with equipment suppliers and cybersecurity specialists. The available information does not confirm full restoration across all affected stores. 

### One recommended next step


**Require joint technical and food-safety approval before reconnecting systems:** verify that remote access to refrigeration controllers is secured and that independent temperature measurements confirm sustained operation within the required storage ranges. This is a recommendation, not a measure confirmed to have been implemented at every site.


## 4. Potential root causes


- **Manipulation of defrost functions:** According to Sabcobel statements reported in the press, attackers remotely activated evaporator defrosting. Leaving this function active for an extended period causes temperatures to rise. This mechanism describes the attack’s effect but does not establish the initial entry point.
- **Older or unpatched controllers:** Kitman Coldtech stated that installations still using outdated or unpatched controllers are being targeted. Access to the control system was confirmed in the Estonian case, but no specific vulnerability was publicly identified.
- **Attribution remains unresolved:** The attackers’ identities, motivations and any coordination across countries remain undetermined in the available reporting.
